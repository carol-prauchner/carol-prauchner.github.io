Curiosities

As I said, here you can find out more about my hobbies and the things I like. For example: one of the things that really interests me is learning new languages. To give you an idea of how much, I've had classes at some point in my life in English, Spanish, Dutch, German, French, Russian, and Mandarin. I also tried to learn a bit of Korean by myself (and I can proudly say that I can already read a bit of Hangul - the Korean alphabet!). However, I don't speak most of them – I wish I could! I also enjoy playing DnD and other puzzles; I can spend a lot of time before bed on my phone just playing. And my taste for music is chaotic! You can read about each one by scrolling down. Have fun!

Languages

🇬🇧 English

I've been learning English since my school years, since it is taught in every Brazilian school. I also took an English course to improve my skills during my undergraduate years because I knew it would be important for my career and I wanted to apply for an internship program.

🇪🇸 Spanish

I learned it while preparing for the university entrance exams. I had to choose one foreign language, and it was the best option to get more points for each correct answer on the exam. So, I ended up having two years of Spanish lessons.

🇳🇱 Dutch

I'm a double-degree PhD candidate at a Dutch university and I lived there for a year as part of my work. So, I took an intensive Dutch course to learn the basics and get to know a bit more of their culture. I can say that I enjoyed this language a lot! However, the sound of "g" is impossible for me!!! I'm still studying this language in my spare time nowadays.

🇩🇪 German

I confess that I don't remember much! My grandparents speak a bit of German, so it was a language that always caught my attention since I was a kid. As a teenager, I took a few months of private lessons. It was a bit hard at first, but it was really nice to see how happy my grandparents were to hear me trying to say things in German to them.

🇫🇷 French

As for French, remember what I said earlier about wanting to apply for an internship? I did it and got accepted to study for a year in Scotland, as part of my undergraduate course. You may wonder what Scotland has to do with learning French. Well, I was pretty advanced in my course and selected all the courses related to my Bachelor's that I could take – and I still had time to fill in my schedule. So, I decided to enroll in French courses! I ended up doing two semesters of French at the University of Aberdeen. It was a challenge at first, to learn a new language through a second one, but it was worth it! I'd say I'm a beginner in French, but I can manage to read scientific papers related to my area of expertise pretty well – and I definitely wouldn't starve in a French-speaking country!

🇷🇺 Russian

This is the language I remember the least! I enrolled for one semester out of curiosity. Because I didn't have many places where I could practice it and couldn't enroll for the classes the following semester because they clashed with some of my master's courses, I ended up forgetting most of it. This was the first language that didn't use the Roman alphabet – it uses the Cyrillic one, which was a nice challenge!

🇨🇳 Mandarin

Last, but not least, Mandarin! I studied it for a year during the COVID-19 pandemic, so I had online classes. In Brazil, people stayed mostly at home for almost two whole years during the pandemic, so this course was really helpful as something to keep me distracted while being at home. This language was a pretty hard challenge to learn! Not only does it not use an alphabet – it uses ideograms – but it also has four different tones! Which is something I didn't really know existed, but now I know that some languages can have more than ten tones! I enjoyed learning this language and I hope I can resume this course at some point. I know some pretty basic sentences, which could maybe help me to survive in China. Writing this made me think that I need to review my notes and books so I can remove the "maybe" from the previous sentence.

Musical Taste: Eclectic!

Listening to music is a part of every day in my life! I like to listen to music while on the bus, in the shower, while studying or working, or when I'm cleaning my place. As for my musical taste, I guess it was pretty influenced by my hobby of learning new languages. If you take a look at my playlists on Spotify, you'll understand what I mean. I created or follow playlists for every genre that I like, such as Brazilian rock, MPB (Música Popular Brasileira), pop, K-pop, C-pop (the latter two are Korean and Chinese pop), indie, Sertanejo, Pagode/Samba (these last two are Brazilian genres), among others. But I also have playlists of songs in specific languages, such as French, Dutch, Chinese, Spanish… So, you see, one hobby affects the other.

As I mentioned before, music helps me with my daily activities or just to pass the time. However, beyond that, it is also a nice and interesting way of practicing new languages, since you can learn new vocabulary and practice listening comprehension. I also practice a bit of speaking by trying to sing along to some songs. And no, I don't sing at all! So, I'm really sorry to my family and my current housemate for all the suffering and ear harm caused!

Dungeons & Dragons

I would like to have written "Role Playing Games" as a title, however, D&D is the only one I've played. I started playing it quite late compared to most people who enjoy RPG – I was in my late twenties. I started playing thanks to an intern I was supervising at ZOOBOT who, after we talked over coffee one day, invited me to try it out when I said I was really curious about it. Since then, I've been playing fairly often with different groups of friends! It's really interesting how I found that so many people I knew for quite some time play RPGs and I didn't know before.

I don't think I can explain everything about RPG or D&D here. But, in general, there is a person who controls the story and each of the other players interprets a specific character that they create. The world of D&D is a fantasy world similar to a medieval era, where creatures of different races and species live. I've played as a druid elf and a druid aasimar. Nowadays, I'm currently playing with two groups, and in both, I play rogue tieflings. If you have no idea of what I'm talking about, you can search online for the D&D Player's Handbook, where you can find the description of races and classes, as well as other rules and topics about it!

I explained a little bit of it, so now I can better explain why I like it. I've always liked reading fantasy books because it's a way of entering new worlds. Playing D&D works the same way for me. Every session, I get to experience the point of view of different characters in completely different worlds. I can exercise my imagination and hang out with friends, which helps a lot to deal with everyday life stress. In the character-building process, I get to think and try to comprehend different points of view while building each character's personality and background story. I believe that this process also helps me become more understanding of people around me, so I can say that RPG can help someone improve certain soft skills that are important in any work area.

Puzzles and Board games

Some friends find it quite amusing that I'm a bit addicted to playing puzzle games – especially jigsaw puzzles – on my phone. I play jigsaw puzzles almost every day before going to bed. I love it! And I've liked jigsaw puzzles since I was a kid. Trying to recognize patterns to fit each piece together is something that really gets me focused. Maybe that's also why I'm building a scientific career. In my work as a PhD candidate in ecology, I get to find and understand patterns, so I can explain certain aspects of the world around us.

Apart from jigsaw puzzles, there are several other games – such as board games – that I enjoy. Those are usually games that involve strategy, like the Game of Thrones board game (I love the game, but I've never watched the series).
